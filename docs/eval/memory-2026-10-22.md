# Memory and performance audit, 2026-10-22

Scope: what the app, the session host (`vibeswitcher-host`), the per-session parsers and the tool servers
(`vibeswitcher-mcp`) hold, and how that grows for a user who runs 10–20 sessions for days. Base: project-lead at
fa9131e. Ranked by impact for that user. Items already fixed, or already listed in `open-2026-10-19.md`, are in
[their own section](#already-fixed-or-listed) and are not reported again.

## How this was measured

- **The real app and sessions, read only.** `ps`, `footprint` and `vmmap --summary` by pid; `lsof` on leaked scratch
  hosts. Nothing was signalled, typed into or restarted. A read-only sampler took `footprint` every 2 min (app, host,
  two lead tool servers) and every 5 min (each session parser) from 02:31 to 04:22. The installed app had been
  relaunched at 02:20 by an install. `heap` was not run on any live process: the lead refused it for the genswap
  lead's tool server, and the permission classifier refused it for the vibeswitcher lead's.
- **Three scratch harnesses**, linked against this worktree's debug build of VibeCore and SwiftTerm, in the session's
  TMPDIR and on a scratch home (`HOME`, `CFFIXED_USER_HOME`, `VIBESWITCHER_HOME`):
  - `termmem` measures a SwiftTerm `Terminal` configured as the parser (5,000 lines) and as the app's view (10,000
    lines). It is fed 20,000 lines of agent-shaped coloured output.
  - `chatgrow` measures a chat left open while its transcript grows: `ChatReader.read` and `ChatLog.append` over the
    last 68 MB (one day) of the vibeswitcher lead's own transcript, in 64 KB polls.
  - `leadloop` runs today's `LeadToolset` in process with a lead grant from an in-memory `LeadVault.Book`. It uses a
    copy of the vibeswitcher board (2.5 MB, 174 tasks) and the largest worker transcript (39 MB), and calls status,
    board, sessions, questions, board(task) and read_session in a loop.
  - One micro-bench (`swiftc -O`) runs the parser link's outbox drain pattern.
- **Mac:** 12 cores. Load was 13–23 at the start and about 9 at the end, so timings are inflated. The memory figures
  don't depend on load.

### 1. Session parsers: 5,000 lines of scrollback each, for the phone's history pages only (P1)

- **What.** Every live session has a parser process. It runs a SwiftTerm `Terminal` with `scrollback: 5_000`. As the
  session prints, the buffer fills to 5,000 lines and stays full. Nothing in the host reads that scrollback except
  the `.history` request (capped at 5,000 lines). Its only client is the phone's history pages.
- **Where.**
  - `Sources/VibeHost/SessionParser.swift:61`: `TerminalOptions(cols:rows:scrollback: 5_000)`.
  - `Sources/VibeHost/SessionParser.swift:111-116`: `history` builds the whole buffer as one string on each request.
  - `Sources/VibeHost/HostServer.swift:393-396`: the `.history` request.
  - `Sources/VibeSwitcher/PhoneAccess.swift:680`: its only client, `hostClient.history(hostID, lines: 5_000)`.
  - The host keeps an 8 MB raw output ring per session as well (`HostServer.swift:16`).
- **Evidence (measured).**
  - **The live parsers** (read-only `footprint`), after the 02:20 host handover restarted them:

    | Time | Parsers | Total |
    |---|---|---|
    | 02:33 | 13 | 250 MB |
    | 03:33 | 13 | 298 MB |
    | 04:19 | 15 | **352 MB** |

    - Each parser is 9–55 MB.
    - The busiest one went 48 → 55 MB. Its `vmmap` shows 467,544 live malloc blocks (37.7 MB), almost all small.
  - **`termmem` on a SwiftTerm `Terminal`:**
    - At 120 columns: 3.2 MB at 1,000 lines, 15.4 MB at 5,000 lines, and flat after that.
    - At 200 columns: 25.2 MB once full.
    - That is about 3.1 KB per line at 120 columns and 5.2 KB at 200.
    - Real agent output, with wide and clustered characters, costs more: the live parsers go past these figures.
- **Impact.** With 20 sessions open for a day, every parser reaches its cap.
  - That is 20 × 15–55 MB = **0.3–1.1 GB**, held in parser processes for one occasionally used phone feature.
  - It comes on top of the host's 8 MB ring per session and the app's own views (#2).
  - It also scales with width: a 200-column window doubles it.
- **Fix sketch.**
  - Cut the parser's scrollback to what the host itself needs, a screen plus a small margin (for example 200 lines).
  - Serve phone history pages on demand instead: feed the session's ring (≤ 10 MB, already in the host) into a
    temporary `Terminal` with 5,000 lines, take the page, and drop the terminal. The phone asks rarely, and the cost
    is paid only then.
  - **Check:** a host test asks for history after 10,000 printed lines and gets the same last 5,000 lines as today.
    A bench (or `VIBESWITCHER_MEASURE_HOST`) shows each parser's footprint under ~6 MB after 20,000 lines at 120
    columns.

### 2. The app's terminal views: 8 kept, 10,000 lines each, parsing on main while hidden (P1)

- **What.** The window keeps the terminal views of the last 8 sessions it showed (LRU). Each one is a full SwiftTerm
  terminal with 10,000 lines of scrollback by default, and up to 100,000 if set in Settings. A view that's off screen
  is only "parked": its layer contents are dropped, but it stays attached to the host. It goes on parsing that
  session's whole output on the main thread. An ended session's view stays until it's evicted, or until the host
  forgets the session 24 h later.
- **Where.**
  - `Sources/VibeSwitcher/MainWindow/TerminalPane.swift`:
    - `:48`: `keepAttached = 8`.
    - `:60`: `scrollbackLines = 10_000`.
    - `:126-130`: `park` keeps the view and its stream.
    - `:236-238`: the output sink feeds `terminal.feed` from the host connection, which is on main.
  - `Sources/VibeSwitcher/SettingsWindow.swift:474`: up to 100,000 lines.
  - `Sources/VibeSwitcher/MainWindow/MainWindowController.swift:185-190`: a view is dropped only when the host no
    longer lists its session.
- **Evidence (measured).**
  - `termmem` with 10,000 lines: 30.7 MB at 120 columns and 50.2 MB at 200 columns once full. At 100,000 lines that
    is ten times as much.
  - So 8 views come to **246–400 MB** at the default.
  - The installed app's footprint (read-only sampler) went 268 MB at 02:31 → 334 at 02:45 → 385 at 03:46 → 434 at
    04:14 → **453 MB at 04:22**. Over about 2 h of use that is a steady ~100 MB/h, with small dips. That is the
    pattern of views filling their scrollback.
  - `vmmap` at 02:30 showed 105 MB of live malloc in 587k blocks. Not measured: how many views were attached; the app
    was in the user's hands.
  - The parked parsing on main is from **code reading**: the sink is a closure on the connection's main-queue
    publisher, and parking doesn't detach it.
- **Impact.** Someone who clicks through 8 or more sessions in a day ends with the app at 0.4–0.6 GB. That's on top
  of the parsers (#1). Up to 8 busy agents' output is parsed on the main thread whether shown or not, so every
  Claude spinner frame in 8 sessions costs main-thread time. That is the same class of cost as the idle-CPU work
  (R3).
- **Fix sketch.**
  - Keep only the shown view plus 2 recent ones attached (`keepAttached` 3).
  - Detach a parked view from the host after 30 s off screen. On return, the host's 1 MB replay
    (`HostedSession.swift:442`) redraws it, as it does for an evicted one today.
  - Drop an ended session's view when its row ends, not 24 h later.
  - Consider a 5,000-line default.
  - **Check:** `--idle-bench --churn` gains a phase that shows 12 sessions that print 20,000 lines each, then
    measures. Assert the closed-window footprint returns to its warm baseline (as the R8 check does), and that with
    the window open on one session the footprint stays under baseline + 3 views. A main-thread CPU sample with 8
    hidden spinner sessions should drop to the shown one's cost.

### 3. An interrupted bench leaves its host and sessions running for good (P2)

- **What.** `--idle-bench` (and `--power`) start a private host whose sessions never end by themselves: a `while :`
  spinner that writes a title every 0.1 s, and `exec /bin/cat` shells. The bench ends the host only in its normal
  `finish()`: it looks for a host process whose parent is the bench. If the bench is killed (a timeout, Ctrl-C, a
  worker's session ended), the host is re-parented to launchd and never exits:
  - Its idle exit needs no live session (`HostServer.swift:687-693`).
  - It never notices that its socket or its scratch home is gone.
- **Where.**
  - `Sources/VibeSwitcher/IdleBench.swift:262-266` (spinner and cat sessions), `:458` (churn shells),
    `:580-588` (`finish` kills by `ppid == getpid()` only, on a normal end).
  - `Sources/VibeSwitcher/PowerBench.swift:93-100` (its host has `--idle-exit 60`, which never fires while its
    sessions live).
  - `Sources/VibeHost/HostServer.swift:681-693` (`tidy`: no check of its parent or socket).
- **Evidence (measured, read only).** Four hosts from the deleted `selecting-text-in-the` worktree's debug build
  started on 10-20 between 15:09 and 15:27. They still ran 35 h later:

  | pid | Sessions | Notes |
  |---|---|---|
  | 88054 | 49 | 48 `/bin/cat`, 1 spinner |
  | 73472 | 49 | |
  | 33160 | 5 | |
  | 55000 | 5 | |

  - Each session has a parser, so that is 108 PTY masters (`lsof`: 49 on 88054 alone) out of `kern.tty.ptmx_max` =
    511.
  - About 220 processes in all.
  - Each spinner had used ~8 min of CPU and each host ~5 min.
  - Their scratch folders (`/tmp/vst.*`, `/tmp/vsh.*`, `/tmp/vsz.*`) were already gone.
  - Related: 718 scratch folders and sockets from test runs and benches sit in `/tmp` (1.1 GB), for example 304
    `vspb.*` from `PowerBench` and 351 `vsh-*.sock`. The data volume had **5.7 GiB free** at 04:30, and the lead's
    `status` already warns about it.
- **Impact.**
  - Each interrupted bench takes up to ~50 PTYs and two processes per session, for good, until reboot.
  - A few more such runs would use up the Mac's PTYs. Then no new session starts, in VibeSwitcher or in Terminal.
  - There is also a small but steady CPU and wakeup cost from the spinners.
  - This affects the developer's Mac (workers run these benches), not end users.
- **Fix sketch.**
  - A host started for a bench or a test exits when its socket path no longer names its own socket (inode check in
    `tidy`), or when the process that started it ends (a `DispatchSource` process watch on its parent, behind a
    `--exit-with-parent` flag the benches pass). It then ends its sessions.
  - The benches pass that flag, and `PowerBench` removes its `/tmp/vspb-*` socket on exit.
  - `scripts/test.sh`'s leftover check (aaf863e) covers only test runs, not benches started by hand.
  - **Check:** a test starts a bench host with the flag, then kills the starter with SIGKILL, and asserts that the
    host and its sessions are gone within one housekeeping interval. A second test removes the socket file and
    asserts the same.

### 4. A tool server that couldn't switch builds kept an old one for days, and grew to 134 MB (P2)

- **What.** The genswap lead's tool server (pid 11179) started on 10-18 at 22:01. That was before in-place switching
  landed (b08f253, 10-18 23:22). It has run that build since. On each install it only tells the lead "VibeSwitcher
  was updated; reconnect the vibeswitcher tools: /mcp" (8 times in its MCP log). Its memory grew to:
  - a 160 MB footprint (peak 187 MB)
  - 134 MB of live malloc in **331,761 blocks**
  - 2 threads, 12 fds, no leaked pipes or threads
- **Comparison.** The other leads' tool servers hold 8.5 MB (TGE, current build) and 32 MB (vibeswitcher). Its
  project's board is only 1.4 MB.
- **Where.**
  - `Sources/VibeCore/MCPServer.swift:81, 97-123, 513`: the switch is attempted only by builds that have it.
  - `Sources/vibeswitcher-mcp/main.swift:286`.
- **Evidence (measured).**
  - `vmmap --summary` of pid 11179: MallocHelperZone 108 MB in 77,705 blocks, DefaultMallocZone 25.8 MB in 254,056
    blocks.
  - Its session made 1,097 tool calls in 3 days, including 65 `merge`, 82 `audit`, 111 `cleanup`, 126 `status` and
    13 `read_session`.
  - **Cause: unknown.** The growth belongs to the 10-18 build. Today's tool servers re-exec on every install, which
    also resets their memory, so the same growth would be hidden today rather than gone.
  - **Scratch repro on today's code** (`leadloop`, 174-task board, 39 MB worker transcript): flat at 77–90 MB of
    malloc over 1,000 rounds (6,000 calls). Today's read paths don't grow; merge, audit and cleanup weren't driven.
    See [the repro](#repro-of-4-on-todays-code).
- **Impact.**
  - A long-lived lead session started before an install of a switching build keeps running old tool code. That
    includes fixed bugs. It also keeps a heap that grows by ~45 MB a day.
  - With 3 leads open for a week that is half a GB, and stale code paths on top.
- **Fix sketch.**
  - The app finds tool servers whose build is older than the installed one and can't switch (no answer to the
    probe). It shows "Lead · genswap runs old tools: reconnect" with a one-click `/mcp` reconnect typed into that
    lead when it's idle, or it ends the old server between calls, since Claude Code restarts it.
  - To name the growth: run `leadloop` (and a variant with `merge`/`audit` on a scratch repo) on the 10-18 build
    and on today's, with `heap` snapshots of the harness before and after. Then compare the top classes.
  - **Check:** after an install, no tool server older than the installed build is still running 10 minutes later
    (a test with a stand-in old build that doesn't answer the probe).

### 5. The parser link copies its whole outbox after every partial write (P3)

- **What.** `ParserLink.flush` writes to the parser's socket. After each partial write it does
  `outbox = Data(outbox.dropFirst(written))`, which copies everything still queued. When a parser starts or
  restarts, or a handover restores sessions, the whole replay (up to 10 MB) is queued at once. It then drains in
  socket-sized writes, so the copying is quadratic. All of it runs on the host's one queue, which every session and
  client shares.
- **Where.**
  - `Sources/VibeHost/SessionParser.swift:409-421`.
  - The replay is queued at `Sources/VibeHost/HostedSession.swift:173, 221, 244`.
  - `ClientConnection` already avoids this with a head index (`ClientConnection.swift:41`).
- **Evidence (measured).** The same pattern in a `swiftc -O` micro-bench takes **0.42 s** for one 10 MB replay at the
  AF_UNIX default of 8 KB per write (1,280 copies). At 64 KB per write it takes 0.16 s. With a head index it takes
  effectively 0 s.
- **Impact.**
  - Every install hands the host over and replays every session into a new parser.
  - With 20 busy sessions (rings near 8–10 MB), the host's queue spends several seconds copying. Meanwhile no input,
    output or request is served for any session.
  - A crashing parser restarts with the same cost each time (≤ 5 a minute).
- **Fix sketch.**
  - Keep a `headWritten` offset as `ClientConnection` does, and compact only when the head passes half the buffer.
  - Or queue the outbox as a list of `Data` chunks.
  - **Check:** a unit test drains a 10 MB outbox through a socketpair with an 8 KB send buffer in under 50 ms. A
    handover test with 10 sessions of 8 MB each logs the host's longest queue stall under 100 ms.

### 6. The open chat's log has no cap, and each append re-indexes all of it on main (P3)

- **What.** While a session is selected, `ChatModel.poll` reads what its transcript gained, about once a second, and
  `ChatLog.append` adds it. Nothing caps the log until the user selects another session or closes the window. Each
  append rebuilds a `Set` of every entry id and re-indexes every entry, on the main thread. The timeline and
  `SentMatch.prompts` also run over all entries.
- **Where.**
  - `Sources/VibeSwitcher/MainWindow/Chat/ChatModel.swift:254-289` (poll), `:152` (timeline), `:587`.
  - `Sources/VibeCore/ChatTranscript.swift:246-248, 272-281` (append and reindex).
  - Freed at `MainWindowController.swift:510` and on `show`.
- **Evidence (measured).** `chatgrow` over the last 68 MB of the vibeswitcher lead's transcript (a day: it grew 217 MB
  from 10-18 20:11 to 10-22 00:47):

  | Transcript read | Entries | Log holds | Append, avg of last 50 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 13.6 MB | 163 | 3.7 MB | 0.23 ms |
  | 68 MB | 686 | **10.6 MB** | **0.83 ms** |

  Both rise linearly with the day. The SwiftUI rows laid out for a growing `shownLimit` while scrolled up were **not
  measured**: that part is code reading only.
- **Impact.** A lead's chat left selected for a week holds ~75 MB, and every poll's append costs ~6 ms on main.
  Below the hang threshold, but it grows with no bound. Lower than first suspected: lead transcripts are mostly hook
  and tool lines the chat drops.
- **Fix sketch.**
  - Keep the newest N entries (for example 2,000), and fold older ones into "Show earlier", which already re-reads
    from disk.
  - Make `append` incremental: keep the id set and tool index as fields, and add only the new entries.
  - **Check:** `chatgrow`-style unit test: after 10,000 appended entries, the log holds ≤ N, and the cost per append
    is flat (the last 50 within 2× the first 50).

### 7. Tool servers hold whole command outputs and whole transcripts in memory (P3)

- **What.**
  - **Commands:** `Shell.run` keeps all of a command's output in one `Data`. `runChecks` then makes a decoded
    string, a trimmed copy and two header-plus-output copies. A merge's or an audit's `swift test` output is held
    4–5 times until the call ends, and the lead receives only its last 1,500 characters.
  - **Transcripts:** `read_session` loads the worker's whole transcript (`Data(contentsOf:)`) and then keeps its last
    2 MB.
- **Where.**
  - `Sources/VibeCore/Worktrees.swift:75-86, 94-150, 655-659`.
  - `Sources/VibeCore/LeadToolset.swift:986-990, 1589`.
- **Evidence.**
  - Worker transcripts on this Mac (the 200 largest): median 4 MB, p90 10 MB, max 39 MB.
  - `leadloop` called `read_session` on the 39 MB one: malloc in use was 80.8 MB after warm-up.
  - The test-output copies are **code reading only**. No merge was run in scratch.
- **Impact.** Spikes rather than growth. A tool server's footprint peaks at transcript size plus 4–5× the test
  output, and macOS keeps much of that peak as dirty memory afterwards. That's tens to a few hundred MB per lead
  during merges. Medium for a Mac with 3 leads merging at once.
- **Fix sketch.**
  - Stream the command's output to the log file it's written to anyway, and keep only a 64 KB ring in memory for
    the summary and failure parsing.
  - Make `read_session` seek to the last 2 MB, as `TranscriptTail` does with its window.
  - **Check:** a test runs `Shell.run` on a command printing 200 MB and asserts peak malloc under 10 MB. A
    `read_session` test on a 100 MB transcript asserts it reads ≤ 2 MB (a counting reader).

### 8. board.json grows forever, and every reader decodes all of it (P3)

- **What.** `tasks`, `updates` and `questions` are never trimmed. A done task keeps its contract, evidence, notes and
  reports. Every `store.load()` and `update` decodes the whole file. So does every `status`, `board`, `read_session`
  and `report` call in each tool server, each change in the app (off main), and the lead server's 30 s records
  timer when `workflow.md` has no `base_branch`. Each `update` re-encodes it with pretty-printing.
- **Where.**
  - `Sources/VibeCore/LeadBoard.swift:1113-1165` (load and update), `:855, 1184` (only reports capped, at 50).
  - `Sources/VibeCore/LeadToolset.swift:220-223` (`workflow` falls back to the board for the base).
  - `Sources/VibeCore/LeadRecords.swift:98-107` (the 30 s timer).
- **Evidence (measured).**
  - vibeswitcher's board: 2,584,043 bytes, 174 tasks (164 done, about 11 KB each), 191 updates (300 KB).
  - genswap's: 1.36 MB at 71 tasks. TGE's: 1.14 MB.
  - `leadloop`: one `lead.base` read (the timer's) takes **11 ms**, so that timer by itself is negligible. A round
    of status + board + sessions + questions + board(task) + read_session takes 338 ms (debug build, load ~9).
- **Impact.**
  - The cost grows linearly with project age, in every tool call and every app refresh. At 1,000 tasks the board is
    ~11 MB, and each call decodes it.
  - Today it's modest; it becomes material in the months ahead.
- **Fix sketch.**
  - At `cleanup` (or after 7 days), move a done task to `.vibeswitcher/history/<yyyy-mm>.jsonl`, keeping on the board
    its slug, title, status, merge commit and score.
  - Trim `updates` to the last 200.
  - `board(task:)` falls back to the history file.
  - **Check:** a test with 1,000 done tasks shows the board under 500 KB after archiving. `board` still answers for
    an archived slug, and `status` stays under 20 ms.

## Looked at, fine

- **Host output rings** are 8 MB, trimmed at 10 MB (`OutputRing.swift:46-50`) and freed when a session ends
  (`HostedSession.swift:407-414`). An ended session keeps only its screen and ≤ 500 lines for 24 h. Measured: the
  real host is 58–81 MB footprint with 79 sessions started in the last 24 h (peak day: 252).
- **Host per-client queues:** 16 MB, then detach and resync; 64 MB of control frames, then close
  (`ClientConnection.swift:9, 70, 77`). The input queue is about 2 MB, and the parser backlog pauses PTY reads at
  4 MB (`HostedSession.swift:55, 79`).
- **Handover:** no fd, master or parser is carried twice (`HostHandover.swift:66`; parsers stopped before the exec).
  The `host.lock.handover` file can be left behind only if the host dies inside the probe window. None is on disk now.
- **App caches:**
  - Pruned to live sessions on each scan: SessionStore's per-tty maps, SessionScanner's caches, `ArgumentCache`,
    `HostClient`'s sinks, replies and screens, and PhoneAccess's caches.
  - The NSCaches for chat text are 8 MB each; thumbnails 150 MB.
  - `UnchangedFiles` caches hold ≤ 512 entries with small values (`TranscriptReader.swift:873`).
  - A few `LeadController` sets (`told`, `seenQuestions`, `answerNotices`) and `SummaryCache` never shrink, but hold
    ~100–300 B per witness event or summary: under 1 MB over weeks. Cosmetic.
- **Timers:** one Heartbeat. The chat timer stops when the chat is hidden. There is one state-folder watcher and no
  per-session watchers. The host has a 30 s housekeeping tick; each session has an exit poll that backs off to 30 s
  and a parser watchdog only while busy. Tool servers have a 1 s stdin poll and, in a lead, the 30 s records timer
  (11 ms per tick, #8).
- **Tool server per-call state:** `running` and `pending` are cleared per call; no thread or pipe leak was seen
  (2 threads and 12 fds on the 3-day server).
- **Disk:**
  - `~/.vibeswitcher` is 62 MB (debug 18, lead 15, host 11, bin 8, attachments 8).
  - Logs are capped: app.log and debug/host.log 1 MB + `.1`, hang reports the newest 10, and the phone audit log
    512 KB + `.1`.
  - `~/.vibeswitcher/host.log` (the host's stderr) and `stats/<day>.json` have no cap, but are 4 KB and 128 KB.
  - The vault (`lead/vault.sealed`, 8 KB) is rewritten on every change, and its per-task records stay until `retire`.
  - The repo's `.vibeswitcher` is 1.7 GB: 1.4 GB of worktrees (5 live, one with a 1.2 GB `.build`) and a 348 MB
    shared module cache. Finished tasks' worktrees are removed by the lead's `cleanup`; none are left over today.
  - Small, never pruned: the `run/*.lock` files (171) and `lead/run/<name>/` launch folders.
- **Main-thread file reads:** `followChat`'s state, inbox, delivered and `chatSeen` reads (1 s, only while the chat
  is visible) and the Team view's 5 s `workflow.md` read are small files. Worth moving off main someday, not material.
