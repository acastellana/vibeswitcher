# VibeSwitcher UI and UX audit, 2026-10-22

This audit was done on the branch `agent/audit-2026-10-22-2`, based on project-lead at **fa9131e**. It ranks the gaps
that are still open across these surfaces:

- the main window: sidebar list, header, chat, terminal pane and the inspector's Team, Overview and Activity tabs;
- the popover, the menu bar and the floating pill;
- Settings;
- the phone web app;
- first run and New Project;
- the lead's views, and the answer and picker cards.

The bar is material issues only. Anything already listed in `docs/eval/open-2026-10-19.md` is not reported again. Most
of that list's UX items have been fixed since: WT-1, PH-1, WC-1, WL-1, WL-3, WI-1, AX-5 and AX-6 (popover, sidebar and
pill). Those fixes were taken as done unless they turned out to be incomplete.

## How this was checked

- **Code reading.** The code was read at the file:line given in each finding. Four readers covered the surfaces
  in parallel: the shell (menu bar, popover, pill, list, header, palette), the chat and its cards, the lead's views
  with Settings, onboarding and New Project, and the phone. Before anything went into the ranking, every reader's
  claim was checked again against the code. Two claims didn't hold up and were dropped:
  - Codex's ⌥↩ "queues but steers": the outbox's queue path wasn't traced to the end.
  - Esc in the composer: what AppKit does by default wasn't confirmed.
- **Renders.** The app's own snapshot tests drew 168 renders on scratch data, in a scratch home from
  `scripts/test.sh` (`*_SNAPSHOT_DIR` into the session's TMPDIR, so not committed). Those renders back the
  "looked at, fine" list. No finding below rests on a picture alone.
  - Run: `scripts/test.sh --filter 'Snapshots|EndedFoldTests|GlossaryTests|PickerCardTests|ChatMessageTextTests|HostRestartTests|StatusRowsPaneTests|TeamFoldTests'`.
  - Summary line: `Test run with 84 tests failed after 92.492 seconds with 1 issue`.
  - The one issue was a timing check: `PickerCardTests.swift:35`, the card took 2.04 s against a 1 s bound, at a load
    of about 18. It isn't a UI defect, but it is a wall-clock bound inside the default correctness suite.
- **Fake-DOM runs.** The phone's `Web/app.js` was run unmodified in node, on a fake DOM, from a scratch copy of the
  `Tests/Web` harness.
- **Live counts.** Two counts come from the user's own files, read only and not quoted:
  - `~/.vibeswitcher/app-status.json` held **14 live sessions** (9 working).
  - The project's `.vibeswitcher/board.json` holds **174 tasks** (164 done).
- **What was never touched.** The installed app was not driven, no window was moved, nothing was typed into a session
  and nothing was installed.

Severity:

- **P0:** loses work or data.
- **P1:** misleads or blocks a frequent path.
- **P2:** misleads or costs time on a common path, or blocks a less common one.
- **P3:** smaller.

### 1. The lead's workers and verifiers count as your sessions everywhere outside the window (P1)

- **What the user sees.** The main window folds a lead's team under the lead. Every other surface treats each worker
  and verifier as one of your own sessions:
  - **Menu bar.** It has a dot per session up to 12, then falls back to counts. With the user's 14 live sessions
    today it already shows counts, so click-a-dot-to-switch is gone. The popover footer still says "Click a dot to
    switch".
  - **Popover.** It lists every worker flat, numbered 1…N, among your own sessions, so 1–9 reach mostly workers.
  - **Floating pill.** It has no cap: one 21 pt dot per session, so a busy day draws a strip several hundred points
    wide, always on top.
  - **Notifications.** "<worker> is done" comes as a banner for every worker that reports, and "may be stuck" or
    "one command running for N min" reminders come for every long build in a worker.
  - **Phone.** The same pushes reach the phone while you're away.
  - **Today ledger.** It books a worker's unseen "done" as time "waiting on you".
- **Where.**
  - The flat list: `SessionStore.swift:258-372` builds `sessions` from every live tty with no role filter.
  - Every surface reads that list: `PopoverView.swift:49`, `StatusStyle.swift:188` (`maxDots` 12 → `counts`),
    `FloatingPanel.swift:96` (`ForEach` over all, no cap), `PhoneAccess.swift:494`.
  - The alerts: `SessionStore.swift:335-356` (`alerts = !viewing && !session.isPaused`) feeds `onAttention`, then
    `AppDelegate.swift:152` `notifier.post`, then `:154` the phone push.
  - The ledger: `SessionStore.swift:382` `recordLedger(result…)`.
  - The role is known: `LeadTeams.role(label:)` (`WindowModel.swift:90`). It is used only by the window.
- **Evidence.** A code reading of the paths above; no other filter exists (grep for `role`/`worker` in
  `SessionStore`, `Notifier`, `FloatingPanel` and `PopoverView`). The live status file shows 14 sessions, with the menu
  bar past the 12-dot limit.
- **Who, how often.** Everyone who runs a lead, all day. With `max_workers` of 3 to 4 plus verifiers, one lead and a
  couple of your own sessions pass 12. Each finished task costs a banner, a sound-less "done" and a green dot until
  you look at a worker you never needed to see.
- **Fix sketch.**
  - Give `Session` a `teamRole` from the host label, as `LeadTeams.role` does.
  - The menu bar, popover, pill and phone show a lead with its team folded in: one dot and row for the lead, a
    "+3" badge, its worst team state as the lead's.
  - Workers' done and stalled states notify nobody, since the lead gets those reports.
  - A worker that **needs you** still alerts, named "<lead> · worker <task> needs you".
  - Ledger time for team members goes under the lead, never "waiting on you".
  - Cap the pill at `maxDots` with the same counts fallback.
- **Check.**
  - A `SessionStore` test with a lead, 4 workers and 2 own sessions: 3 dots, and 0 `onAttention` calls for a worker
    turning done, but 1 for a worker turning needs-input.
  - A `StatusSnapshots` render with 14 sessions, 10 of them team members, that stays in dots mode.
  - A `FloatingDots` render with 20 sessions within the cap.

### 2. With the Mac locked, the phone offers answers it will refuse (P1)

- **What the user sees.**
  1. The phone is notified only while you're away, and a locked screen counts as away. A push says "X needs your
     input".
  2. The phone opens the session with live option buttons, quick replies and a composer.
  3. Every tap or send comes back "The Mac is locked." Window sessions are refused too, although nothing is focused
     or typed outside their own PTY.
  4. Nothing on the list or the session says beforehand that the Mac is locked.
  - The rule itself is deliberate (README, "nothing is typed while the Mac is locked"). The gap is that the UI invites
    the action and then refuses it, on the phone's main path.
- **Where.**
  - Away includes locked: `UserPresence.swift:6-9`. Pushes go only while away: `PhoneAccess.swift:1135`.
  - Refusals: `PhoneAccess.swift:838-840` (window sessions, 423), `:881` and `:940` (Terminal sessions);
    `RemoteInput.swift:73,85`.
  - `stateObject` (`PhoneAccess.swift:492-535`) carries no lock field, and `Web/app.js` has no `locked` state.
- **Evidence.** Code reading. The push gate and the input gate use the same lock bit, so "notified, then refused" is
  the normal sequence, not an edge case.
- **Who, how often.** Every phone user who locks the Mac on leaving it, which is the default for most, on every
  prompt answered from the phone.
- **Fix sketch.**
  - Add `"locked"` to `/api/state`. While it's set:
    - the session view shows a banner, "Your Mac is locked: answers wait until you unlock it";
    - the option buttons and quick replies are disabled;
    - the composer keeps its draft.
  - The push body says "(Mac locked)" when it is.
  - Whether window sessions should accept input while the Mac is locked is the user's call. It's a security trade
    (no focus change, but remote control of a locked machine), so it's noted here, not recommended.
- **Check.**
  - A Swift test that `stateObject` carries `locked` from `screenLocked`.
  - A `Tests/Web` fake-DOM test: with `locked: true` the menu buttons are disabled and the banner is shown; with
    `false` they're enabled.

### 3. A digit typed into an empty composer answers the open prompt at once (P1)

- **What the user sees.** A permission prompt or question is up, and the composer is empty. You start a message with
  a digit ("1 more thing…", "2 files left"). The first keystroke is taken as option 1 or 2 and typed into the
  terminal at once; window sessions get no confirmation step. Option 1 is usually "Yes". A plain ↩ in the empty box
  confirms whichever option is highlighted.
  - This is what WC-1 asked for ("as in the terminal"). In the terminal, though, the prompt is on screen. Here:
    - the card is the last item of the scrolled conversation;
    - a menu appearing doesn't scroll to it;
    - the placeholder still says "Message Claude… / for commands".
- **Where.**
  - The key path: `ComposerView.swift:636` `keyDown` → `cardKey` (`:150-154`) → `AnswerCardKeys.take`
    (`ChatView.swift:1151-1173`) → `AnswerKeys.action` `.digit` → `.choose(n-1)` (`:1103-1104`).
  - The card sits in the scroll content (`ChatView.swift:206-213`). The view scrolls only when `log.entries.count`
    changes (`:275-277`).
  - The placeholder: `ComposerView.swift:71-73`.
- **Evidence.** Code reading of the key path. `AnswerKeysTests` covers the mapping, but nothing covers "typing a
  message while a prompt is up".
- **Who, how often.** Every window-session user, on any prompt, whenever a message begins with 1–9 or ↩ is pressed
  in an empty box. It's not frequent per prompt, but the cost is an unintended approval.
- **Fix sketch.**
  - The card takes 1–9 and ↩ only while it's at least partly on screen (`RowTops` and `ScrollProbe` already know).
  - When a menu appears and the view was at the bottom, scroll the card into view.
  - While the card holds the keys, the placeholder reads "1–3 answer the question above · type a letter to write a
    message".
- **Check.** Extend `AnswerKeysTests` with the visibility input. Add a `ChatSyncTests` case: card scrolled off, "1"
  typed, the terminal gets nothing and the composer holds "1".

### 4. A message held for a question waits until you look at the chat again (P2)

- **What the user sees.**
  1. A message sent while a question is up shows "waits until the question is answered".
  2. The card offers "Answer in Terminal", which switches the pane to the terminal.
  3. You answer there, and the agent goes idle.
  4. The held message isn't sent until you switch back to Chat (or the window comes back into sight), so the agent
     sits waiting for an instruction you already gave. Selecting another session strands it the same way.
- **Where.**
  - Held messages are released only from `ChatSync.read` → `releaseHeld` (`ChatSync.swift:281-283`, `:583`).
  - Reads run only from `followChat`, which needs `chatInSight` (`MainWindowController.swift:488-495`). Its timer is
    cancelled when the chat isn't shown (`:363-368`).
  - "Answer in Terminal": `ChatView.swift:872`.
- **Evidence.** Code reading. `PickerCardTests.noReadsWhileTheChatIsOutOfSight` and
  `aHeldMessageStaysHeldAcrossSwitchingAwayAndBack` pin "no reads out of sight" and "held while away". Neither checks
  release after an answer given in the terminal.
- **Who, how often.** Anyone who answers a question in the terminal pane after queuing a follow-up. It's the path the
  card itself offers.
- **Fix sketch.** While any message is held for a session, keep a light watch on that session's screen whatever is
  shown. It's one host-screen read every 1–2 s, only while something is held, so the "nothing out of sight" rule still
  holds otherwise. Release on the same "clear for `releaseAfter`" rule.
- **Check.** A `PickerCardTests`/`ChatSyncTests` case: hold a message, show the terminal, clear the menu on the fake
  host, and expect the message typed within 3 s. Count the reads with nothing held: still zero.

### 5. A failed merge approval is reported to a card that's already gone (P2)

- **What the user sees.** You click "Approve <sha>" on a held-merge question, and the card leaves. If the approval
  couldn't be recorded, the notice is written but never shown. That happens when no task's branch is at that commit
  any more (the worker committed again), or when the vault write failed. You believe the merge is approved, and merge
  keeps refusing it. "Already answered" and "no longer on the board" are lost the same way.
- **Where.**
  - The notices are set at `LeadController.swift:556-559` and `:564-572`.
  - They are drawn only by `LeadQuestionStack` (`LeadViews.swift:185-187`), over `board.questionStack()`. That stack
    holds open questions plus those withdrawn or auto-decided in the last 30 min (`LeadBoard.swift:902-911`), never
    answered or missing ones.
  - `ClosedQuestionRow` takes no notice.
- **Evidence.** Code reading. `LeadApprovalAnswerTests.swift:94` asserts the dictionary entry, not that it's shown.
- **Who, how often.** Users with `merge_policy: ask_*`, on each approval that misses (a branch that moved since the
  question was asked is the likely one).
- **Fix sketch.**
  - An answered question with a notice stays in the stack, collapsed, with the notice in red, until dismissed.
  - An approval that wasn't recorded also posts a notification, "Merge not approved: …".
- **Check.** A `LeadQuestionSnapshots` render of an answered question with the approval-failed notice. A unit test
  that `questionStack` (or its view model) keeps a question that has a notice.

### 6. The Team strip over the lead's chat: every task ever, old words, and chips that throw you out (P2)

- **What the user sees.** Above a lead's chat, `TeamStrip` draws:
  - **The counts in the board's raw words.** "2 planned", "164 done", "1 failed", where the Team view, after WL-3,
    says "Queued" and "Merged".
  - **Wrong states.** A task whose worker ended or is waiting on you still shows as an orange "working" chip, while
    the Team view says "Worker ended" or "Needs you".
  - **Every task ever.** On this project that's 174 chips in a horizontal scroll with no scroll indicator.
  - **Chips that throw you out.** Clicking a merged task's chip selects its worker, which is gone from the host. The
    selection then falls to the first row of the list, out of the lead.
- **Where.**
  - The labels: `LeadViews.swift:20-32` ("Planned", "Done").
  - The strip: `LeadViews.swift:41-99` (raw `shownStatus`, `board.tasks` unfiltered, `showsIndicators: false`, a
    colour dot with no state in its label).
  - The click: `ChatView.swift:27-29` → `WindowModel.select(hostID:)` (`:140`). The fallback is at `:135-137`.
- **Evidence.**
  - Code reading. `board.json` here: 174 tasks, 164 done.
  - The snapshot `QUESTION/questions-team-strip-*` covers only the empty board ("No tasks yet").
- **Who, how often.** Every lead user, every time the lead's chat is open. It gets worse as a project ages.
- **Fix sketch.**
  - Build the strip from `TeamRows.rows`: the same words, marks and live states as the Team view.
  - Show only unfinished tasks, plus "Merged 164" as a link to the Team tab's fold.
  - Disable a chip whose session is gone; it opens the task in the Team tab instead.
- **Check.**
  - A `GlossaryTests` entry covering the strip's words.
  - A snapshot with 30 tasks (3 live, one with an ended worker).
  - A test that a chip for a removed worker doesn't change `selectedID` away from the lead.

### 7. A phone gets unpaired by a restart, silently, and the way back is described wrongly (P2)

- **What the user sees.**
  - **Unpaired by a restart.** Phone Access restarts itself after two failed health checks, for example after a
    Tailscale change. Any request in flight then (the session view polls every 1.5 s) is answered **401 "this device
    isn't paired"**. The phone treats a 401 as "forget my token" and falls back to the pairing screen. Turning Phone
    Access off on the Mac does the same to a phone that's polling.
  - **No reason given.** The pairing screen shows no reason.
  - **A blank list from a notification.** If the token is gone, tapping a notification (`#s=ttys…`) runs
    `route()`'s catch, which shows the list over the pairing screen: an empty list with no message.
  - **Wrong directions.** The pairing screen points to "VibeSwitcher › ⚙︎ › Phone Access… › Pair a phone". The ⚙︎
    menu has no such item; it's Settings › Phone › "Pair a Phone…".
  - Re-pairing needs someone at the Mac, which is exactly what the phone is for when you aren't there.
- **Where.**
  - The 401: `PhoneAccess.swift:410-413` (`state == .on` or 401). The restart: `:343` → `start()` sets `.starting`
    (`:182`). `RemoteServer.stop()` cancels only the listener (`RemoteServer.swift:94-97`).
  - The phone side: `Web/app.js:63-65` (401 → `forgetToken`), `:94-98` (no reason), `:1105` (`.catch(() =>
    show('list'))`).
  - The directions: `Web/index.html:29`, against `PopoverView.swift:237-244` and the Settings Phone tab.
- **Evidence.** Code reading. The fake-DOM run after a revoked token: `token: null | pair hidden: true | list hidden:
  false | empty hidden: true`, and `pairError` empty.
- **Who, how often.** Phone users. It's rare per request but likely across a Tailscale reconnect while a session is
  open, and the cost is high (you're away, and the phone is unusable until you're back).
- **Fix sketch.**
  - When the state isn't `.on`, answer `503 "Phone access is restarting"`. Keep 401 for a removed or unknown device
    only.
  - `forgetToken(reason)` fills `#pairError`.
  - The catch at `:1105` shows the list only while a token is held.
  - One shared string, "VibeSwitcher › Settings… › Phone".
- **Check.**
  - A Swift test with `handleForTests`: flip the state to `.starting` mid-read, and the status is 503.
  - A `Tests/Web` test: a revoked token shows the pairing screen with its reason, and never a blank list.

### 8. Export Chat and chat search cover only the last 4 MB, without saying so (P2)

- **What the user sees.** A chat opens on the transcript's last 4 MB, and "Show earlier" pages back. Export Chat…
  writes only what's loaded, so the exported file silently starts mid-conversation, and a failed write is dropped.
  ⌘F says "No matches" for anything before the loaded part. Long tool output reaches 4 MB quickly, so this hits lead
  sessions most.
- **Where.**
  - Export: `MainWindowController.swift:797-804` (`ChatExport.markdown(chat.log.entries…)`, `try? … write`).
  - The page size: `ChatModel.swift:136` (`pageBytes` 4 MB) and `:697`.
  - The search counter: `ChatView.swift:710`.
- **Evidence.** Code reading.
- **Who, how often.** Anyone exporting or searching a long session, and lead sessions in particular.
- **Fix sketch.**
  - Export reads the whole transcript off the main thread and shows an alert when the write fails.
  - While `hasEarlier` is set, the search bar says "Searching the loaded part · Load all", and the counter says so.
- **Check.** A test on an 8 MB fixture transcript: the exported Markdown holds the first prompt, and a search for it
  shows the hint, not a bare "No matches".

### 9. Review rows hide why: the worker's old checkpoint covers "Needs audit" and audit progress (P2)

- **What the user sees.**
  - **The reason is covered.** In the Team view, a task in review reads, under its title, the worker's last
    checkpoint ("Done: …"). It doesn't read what is happening now:
    - why it **Needs audit** (its branch touches sensitive paths, so merge refuses it until an audit passes);
    - "Waiting for the audit";
    - the verifier's step;
    - "3 finders, 1 reported".
  - **"Ready to merge" is ambiguous.** It looks identical for a task that passed an audit and for one with review
    level none, which was never audited. The difference shows only in the expanded details.
- **Where.**
  - The line: `TeamRows.swift:96-101` (`summary ?? checkpoint?.text ?? activity`). The review cases set `activity`
    at `:274-296`.
  - "Needs audit" has no `agent`, so it has no summary either.
  - "Ready to merge" is set at both `:271-273` and `:279-282`. `isLive` is false for `.merge` (`:79`).
- **Evidence.** Code reading. `TeamRowsTests.swift:201` asserts `.activity`, not the shown line. The busy snapshot
  (`TEAM/busy-team-*`) has its review task without checkpoints, so it renders correctly and hides the case.
- **Who, how often.** Every reviewed task with checkpoints, which is most of them, whenever AI summaries are off or
  not yet written. "Needs audit" is the one state there that asks for action.
- **Fix sketch.**
  - In the review group, prefer `activity` over `checkpoint`.
  - Show "Ready to merge · no audit" (or a distinct mark) for review none. Its spoken label says the same.
- **Check.** A `TeamRowsTests` case: a "Needs audit" task with checkpoints, and `line(summary: nil)` holds the
  reason. A case where the two "Ready to merge" rows differ in text and spoken label.

### 10. The battery limit is described as a rule, but it's only a message to the lead, and the Team view never shows it (P2)

- **What the user sees.**
  - **The setting promises a rule.** Settings › Project lead says that on battery under the set level "the lead
    starts no workers or audits and merges nothing".
  - **Nothing enforces it.** What happens is that one witness message is sent to each running lead when the hold
    begins and when it ends, and the lead is asked to comply. `start_worker`, audits and merge don't check it. The
    Mac-load limit, by contrast, is refused in the tool.
  - **Nothing shows it.** The Team view has lines for Mac load and keep-awake, but none for "held on battery", so
    queued tasks just sit there with no reason shown.
- **Where.**
  - The promise: the `SettingsWindow.swift:179` footer.
  - The message: `LeadController.swift:922-925` `lookAtPower`, and the prompt text at `LeadWorkflow.swift:646-652`.
  - The status line only: `LeadToolset.swift:2351`. There is no `heldUnder` check anywhere in `LeadToolset`.
- **Evidence.** Code reading. A grep for `heldUnder` finds it in `PowerSource.swift`, `LeadWitness.swift` and the
  status text only.
- **Who, how often.** Laptop users who set the limit, whenever they work on battery under it.
- **Fix sketch.**
  - Enforce it where the load limit is enforced: `start_worker`, the audit and merge refuse while `heldUnder` is
    set, saying why.
  - A Team view line, "On battery 42 %: holding new starts (your 50 % limit)".
  - If enforcing is not wanted, the setting's text should say "asks the lead to hold".
- **Check.** A `LeadToolsetTests` case: `start_worker` is refused with a faked held `PowerState`. A `TeamSnapshots`
  render with the line.

### 11. On Android, Back closes the phone app from a session (P2)

- **What the user sees.** The phone app installs as a standalone app. Opening a session only replaces the URL, so
  the system Back gesture in a session leaves the app instead of returning to the list. iOS standalone apps have no
  Back gesture, so this is an Android issue.
- **Where.** `Web/app.js:340` and `:1113` call `history.replaceState` only. There is no `popstate` or `hashchange`
  listener in `Web/`. `manifest.webmanifest` sets `"display": "standalone"`.
- **Evidence.** Code reading (grep).
- **Who, how often.** Android users, on every session they open.
- **Fix sketch.** `pushState` in `openSession`, with a `popstate` handler that shows the list. Prev and next keep
  `replaceState`, so Back returns to the list, not through every session.
- **Check.** A `Tests/Web` test: open a session, dispatch `popstate`, and `#list` is shown. Then by hand on an
  Android phone.

### 12. Accessibility and keyboard: no announcements, a phone list rebuilt every 3 s, Team rows unreachable by keyboard (P3)

- **What the user sees.**
  - **VoiceOver.** It hears nothing when:
    - an answer card appears;
    - a reply finishes;
    - "That question is gone" or "Not delivered…" is set.
  - **Unlabelled prompts.** Your own messages carry no "You" label. Who said what is known only from a separate
    AgentMark element.
  - **The phone list.** It is rebuilt every 3 s with `replaceChildren`, even when nothing changed. A screen-reader's
    focus on a row is destroyed each time, and a tap that straddles a rebuild is lost.
  - **Team rows.** Task rows and decision rows open on a tap gesture only. Neither Tab nor Full Keyboard Access
    reaches them, and "Open worker" exists only inside the expanded row. Task rows do have a VoiceOver default
    action.
- **Where.**
  - No `AccessibilityNotification` or `NSAccessibility.post` anywhere in `Sources/`.
  - The prompt views: `ChatMessageText.swift:849` and `ChatView.swift:511`.
  - The phone list: `Web/app.js:115` and `:194`.
  - The Team rows: `TeamViews.swift:77-83` and `:377-381`.
- **Evidence.** Code reading (grep for the APIs, read of the views). That VoiceOver can activate a decision row is
  unverified.
- **Who, how often.** VoiceOver and keyboard-only users, constantly. For everyone else, the lost taps on the phone,
  rarely.
- **Fix sketch.**
  - Post `.announcementRequested` when a card appears, a reply ends or a notice is set.
  - Label prompts "You, <time>".
  - Key the phone rows by `sid` and update them in place.
  - Make the Team row headers plain-style `Button`s.
- **Check.**
  - An accessibility-tree test on a chat snapshot (prompt labelled "You").
  - A `Tests/Web` test that a row node survives two polls with no change.
  - A focus-order check in `AccessibilityLintTests` that flags `onTapGesture` without `.focusable` or a Button.

## Smaller, not ranked

These are real but under the bar, or only on a less common path:

- **The phone's offline banner slides under the sticky header** once the list is scrolled (`Web/style.css:23`,
  `:156`, both `top: 0`, the header on top). A long, scrolled list of frozen "Working 3m" rows then looks live.
- **The Activity tab scrolls to the bottom on every change** (`InspectorView.swift:144`, unconditional
  `scrollTo("end")`). Earlier lines of a working session can't be read.
